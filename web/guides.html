01

Source

Add a URL or a file

Everything starts in the add bar on the PeakCut dashboard.

  1. Sign in and open the app. Create an account if this is your first visit; confirm your email if the app asks you to.
  2. Paste a link into the field that says “Paste a YouTube, Twitch or Kick video link”. A replay/VOD works as well as a finished upload.
  3. Or add your own video: click Upload video and choose a file, or drop the file onto the add bar. PeakCut shows upload progress and the file size before it starts.
  4. File it under a creator with “File under creator” if you already have one. This keeps that creator's style and posting rules with the video.
  5. Click “Make clips”. The job moves through transcribing, judging and rendering; the Jobs view shows the current stage.

02

Options

Set clip options before rendering

Click Options in the add bar to open the full panel. The defaults suit most streams, but every value stays editable.

  1. Spoken language picks the speech model. English is ready by default; other languages need their model added once in Settings → Speech models.
  2. Clips: set “How many”, “Shortest” and “Longest” (15–60 seconds by default). The stepper fields accept your own range.
  3. Big text on every clip is optional. Leave it empty to let AI write a different hook for each clip; type a line when a campaign needs the same words everywhere.
  4. Description for every clip is optional too. When set, it is posted exactly as typed; otherwise AI writes one per clip.
  5. Caption style — Bounce, Hype or Clean — and Layout — blurred fill, zoomed in, fill screen, keep left/right, black bars, split screen, gameplay + facecam or stacked.
  6. Video quality is 720p by default; 1080p is sharper and takes about twice as long. Part of the video limits work to a “From” and “To” minute range.

03

AI configuration

Bring your own key

Open Settings → AI. PeakCut shows “Ready” with your provider and model once the key works.

  1. Choose an AI service from the list: Gemini, OpenAI, Grok, Claude, OpenRouter, Groq, Ollama or a custom OpenAI-compatible service.
  2. Paste your key into the Key field. It is stored on your server, never in a browser page or in a clip.
  3. Custom service: fill in Address with the endpoint that answers your requests, then type the model name(s). “Find models” queries that endpoint with the saved key.
  4. Models accept a comma-separated list: the first is used and a second can act as a fallback. A fast, inexpensive model is the right choice.
  5. Click “Save and test”. The connection test reports success or failure in the panel; “Test again” re-checks an existing setup.

You pay that provider directly. PeakCut has no AI invoice. AI calls are listed with creator, video and token counts in the usage view so you can see what your key spent.

04

Clip review

Open, edit and re-make

When a job finishes, open it and select a clip. The editor shows the rendered video on the left and every editable field on the right.

  1. Read the score badges. “Words” is the transcript judge; “Reaction” is the loudness/chat judge. A hand-made clip says “Clipped by hand”.
  2. On the video: change Big text, toggle Generate captions, pick another Caption style or Layout, and adjust Start and End in seconds.
  3. Post text: edit Title, Description and Hashtags, or click “Write again with AI” for a fresh suggestion.
  4. Click “Save and re-make clip” to render your changes from the same source.
  5. Take the clip with you: Download the MP4, download Subtitles (.srt), or Copy post text for another tool.

05

Creators & live

Watch a creator go live

Creators keep separate channels, styles, AI rules, posting accounts and results. Open a creator's Setup tab to configure them.

  1. Their stream: add the creator's channel and tick Record automatically when they go live. PeakCut checks straight away, then every two minutes.
  2. Language they speak is used for live recordings and becomes the default for that creator's videos. Add the matching speech model first if it is not English.
  3. How the AI decides: the words judge reads only speech; the reaction judge sees only loudness and chat activity. Choose “Check the live stream every” 1–10 minutes, the minimum score, the clip-per-hour cap and how often to check.
  4. Trigger words such as “clip that, clip it” force a clip of the last 30 seconds from the transcript, without an extra AI call. “Minutes of live stream to keep” sets the rolling buffer.
  5. Clips: caption style, layout, shortest/longest length, clips per video and the minutes of live stream kept are stored per creator.

Only clip what you may clip. PeakCut is a tool; you are responsible for permission to record and repost a creator's content.

06

Publishing

Post or queue a clip

Connect your accounts once in Settings, then choose where each clip goes.

  1. Settings → Accounts: connect YouTube for Shorts and Instagram for Reels. Instagram needs a Creator or Business account; until Meta reviews the app, only added testers can connect. Google may show a warning until the app is verified, and uploads stay private.
  2. In a clip's editor tick the accounts you want and click Post now or Add to queue. A confirmation appears before an immediate post.
  3. Or automate it: a creator's Posting card has “Post new clips automatically”, a minimum score, minutes between posts per account, a daily cap, post privacy and a line added to every post (for example a credit).
  4. Activity shows queued and posted clips with status, account, views and any error, so a failed post does not disappear silently.
